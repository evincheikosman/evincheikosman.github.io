RITE

Risk Intake and Triage Tool

An AI-native intake system that replaced 15+ fragmented internal tools with a single submission path — automatically triaging risk signals, routing them to the right system, and closing the accountability loop end-to-end.

Timeline Under 2 weeks
Production Diffs 62+
Rollout Phased · company-wide
Status Launched & live

⚠ The Problem

Across Meta's integrity organization — and the company more broadly — risk signals, pressures, and emerging compliance issues had no home. They surfaced in DMs, got buried in email threads, stalled in siloed conversations, or never got logged at all. When something did get submitted, stakeholders had to navigate 15+ internal tools to figure out where it even belonged — a burden that required institutional knowledge most people didn't have. Signals slipped through the cracks, patterns went undetected, and the org had no way to see what was coming, let alone act on it proactively.

⚙ What I Built

RITE became the single front door for all risk signal intake — one place to submit, regardless of signal type or destination. Users didn't need to know which internal tool to use. They just submitted through RITE, and the AI handled everything else.

  • Guided submission wizard with AI-powered signal classification — identifies signal type, assesses priority, and determines the correct downstream system automatically
  • 15+ internal tool integrations — AI auto-creates the task or account in the appropriate downstream system (policy tracker, regulatory tool, compliance system, etc.) on the user's behalf
  • Closed accountability loop — submissions remain visible and trackable in RITE after routing, so nothing falls out of sight once triaged
  • Pattern detection and alerting — AI flags when a cluster of related signals is emerging and surfaces it to the internal team for review
  • Admin triage queue with point-of-contact assignment, status tracking, and SLA tiers (P0/P1/P2)
  • Auto-generated discussion threads per signal, keeping context co-located with the record
  • Full audit trail from submission through resolution
  • Escalation integration — signals flagged as high-priority can be raised directly into the IRC Escalation Hub for cross-functional review
  • Companion Triage Playbook — an interactive site walking through the signal lifecycle

◎ Technical Integrations

  • LLM / AI classification engine — signal categorization, priority assessment, and downstream routing logic at intake
  • 15+ internal tool API integrations — automated task and account creation in the correct system based on signal type

  • Google Chat API — auto-generated discussion threads per signal submission
  • IRC Escalation Hub integration — direct escalation path for high-priority signals

✦ What I Learned

Hiding complexity is a product decision. I could have trained every stakeholder at Meta to know which of 15+ internal tools to use for which type of signal. Instead I built a system that absorbed that burden entirely — submit once, the AI routes everything. The tool that does the least in a user's hands is often the one doing the most underneath. That principle now shapes how I think about every product I build.

The second lesson was about compounding systems. RITE didn't just solve intake — it created the foundation for pattern detection, proactive escalation, and org-wide visibility that hadn't existed before. When you design the front door well, everything downstream gets better. A well-scoped V1 is worth more than a perfect V3 that ships too late.

The hardest design decision wasn't technical — it was the routing taxonomy. For the AI to route consistently, I had to model 15+ internal tools precisely enough that their boundaries were unambiguous. That turned out to be organizational work: most of those tools had overlapping ownership that nobody had formally mapped. The AI routing was easy once the taxonomy existed. The prerequisite alignment was not.
